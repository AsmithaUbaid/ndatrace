NDATrace: Does Additional AI Complexity Earn Its Place?

A business case for bounded, evidence-grounded NDA review assistance, tested through controlled engineering experiments.

Ubaidulla Asmitha · Group B

1Business problem and contribution

Tina, a legal-operations analyst, checks vendor NDAs against standard confidentiality requirements before counsel approves them. Obligations may be paraphrased, qualified by exceptions or spread across clauses, so keyword search can locate language without resolving its meaning. Commercial tools such as Ironclad, Luminance and Kira already support AI-assisted review; I test how to treat incomplete or conflicting evidence. NDATrace classifies each requirement as Entailment, Contradiction or NotMentioned and quotes evidence for Tina to check before recording a decision. It replaces an unaided first reading with verification of specific findings, not legal judgment. Survey evidence [1] gives workload context, not proof of savings from this prototype.

I tested whether that assistance merits its AI complexity rather than assuming it does.

2Solution and design decisions

I compared three approaches: a non-AI keyword-rule baseline, a full-context language model (FULL) that reads the whole NDA, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), which retrieves selected clauses before interpretation. The implemented design separates semantic judgment from predictable controls (Figure 1, Table 1). Project-owned code chunks, retrieves, checks that cited quotations exist in the source, applies limits and security flags, and records reviewer decisions. Only interpretation is rented through one GPT-5-mini call. Renting avoids training and hosting costs but adds provider dependence; controlling retrieval bounds model context but makes an omitted clause a new failure mode. Source validation checks provenance, not whether a quotation supports the conclusion.

I began with a rule-based keyword baseline: the cheapest way to test whether AI adds value. Before building retrieval, I ran an oracle experiment giving four models the gold evidence directly. GPT-5-mini interpreted the evidence substantially better than the tested local alternatives, particularly for contradictions (Figure 2). Given the whole NDA on the official TEST set, the local model also fell below the keyword rules. I then tested prompting, chunking, retrieval and reranking before comparing complete architectures. These stages answered different questions on separate populations and are not one pooled benchmark (Table 3).

Experiments also showed where complexity was unnecessary. Very large chunks inflated recall by approaching whole-document coverage, not by focusing evidence. Hybrid retrieval led before reranking, but BM25 and dense candidates reached the same Recall@5 afterward (Figure 3). I chose BM25 with reranking because an embedding index showed no final-stage benefit.

4Measured results and trade-offs

The rule-based system remains the project's non-AI baseline and answers the first question: did adding semantic AI help where keyword matching cannot interpret reliably? Both LLM approaches improved substantially on rules (Table 3, Table 4); RAG's measured gain over Rule is +16.6 risk-sensitive-recall points. Separately, the proposal's original pre-registered target was a five-point gain for RAG over FULL. The measured RAG-over-FULL gain is only +1.2 points (69.1% to 70.3%), so that target was not achieved. FULL remains the stronger quality-reference configuration on Joint correctness, while RAG is the selected interactive runtime. Joint correctness matters here because a correct label backed by the wrong clause is not a dependable review finding. Risk-sensitive recall is the macro-average of Contradiction and NotMentioned recall, so Contradiction counts equally; a pooled recall would be dominated by NotMentioned, where the rule system scores highest by defaulting to that label.

RAG's measured benefit was lower inference cost, not better evidence quality. Its accuracy difference from FULL was not statistically significant, while FULL's Joint advantage was (McNemar p=0.0047). In the base cost-to-serve scenario FULL is also cheaper end to end (Section 6). Keeping RAG is therefore an engineering decision, not a measured win: retrieval caps what the model sees and does not require the whole contract to fit in context, properties I did not test on longer contracts. Reviewer-time measurements and longer-contract tests must decide which architecture is economically preferable; this is not a deployment recommendation.

5Rejected complexity and residual failures

Some failures appeared recoverable through a second search, so I tested three capped agent variants (Figure 5). The selective agent made no tool calls; prompting it to investigate increased tool use without producing useful recoveries. Automatic routing, my stand-in for abstention, also missed its targets: rule-model disagreement would escalate 51.4% of cases to catch 82.5% of failures, exceeding the 40% ceiling, and misses errors on which both systems agree. I therefore rejected both additions: the prototype does not abstain automatically, and human review is mandatory.

Most residual failures are interpretive, not retrieval gaps, with only a small share retrieval-limited (Figure 6). The largest single pattern is over-inference: most of RAG's label errors (330 of 486) are NotMentioned requirements judged supported or contradicted, as FULL's also are, which gives a reviewer false assurance. Both architectures also sometimes misread negations or exceptions despite receiving the right text. RAG sometimes omitted relevant spans or failed to cite retrieved evidence; FULL could be distracted by irrelevant permissive language elsewhere. More context or agent activity alone therefore does not address the largest observed problem: interpreting the supplied clauses correctly.

6Business feasibility and risk

A cheaper API call does not necessarily mean a cheaper review (Table 5, Figure 7). Every finding still requires human verification, errors may trigger additional investigation, and the labor figures are assumptions, not measured savings. In the base scenario, FULL's higher evidence correctness more than offsets RAG's lower inference price; without a timed reviewer study and operational costs, I cannot claim a return on investment.

RAG's savings were smaller than its context reduction suggested: at the recorded GPT-5-mini prices ($0.25 input, $2 output per million tokens; OpenRouter, checked 22 September 2026), output tokens cost eight times as much per token as input and neither architecture reduced them, so halving input tokens did not come close to halving inference cost (Table 5).

Security results (Table 6) come from two populations that should not be merged. The paired attack experiment tested the FULL-context arm: Joint correctness fell from 85% to 75% (20 pairs). A separate check of the selected RAG path used seven synthetic attack cases; one succeeded, a request to return the whole context as evidence. Both samples are small and descriptive. In the FULL-context experiment, one injected passage was returned as evidence and passed source validation because the text existed in the document: provenance does not establish trustworthiness. Cost limits and an injection guard addressed specific risks; detection remained incomplete. Human review provides a decision point, not a proven defense against adversarial text or ordinary, plausible but unsupported findings.

7Critical evaluation and future direction

Evaluation critique

This evaluation supports controlled architectural comparisons, not autonomous legal decisions. Historical access to TEST prevents claiming perfect blindness, though final-protocol choices were not tuned on TEST outcomes. Separately, ContractNLI's public agreements and fixed requirements may not represent longer confidential NDAs. Finally, Joint correctness checks overlap with annotated spans, not whether a finding is legally adequate.

Difficulties overcome

A $10 OpenRouter budget was the binding constraint. The plan was a hosted subsample alongside local-model coverage; I instead completed paired FULL and RAG runs over the full TEST population, consuming most of the key's remaining budget. The record does not show why I departed from the plan, so I report the choice without a justification. Calibration also caught two evaluation risks, exact-character quotation matching that unfairly rejected formatting variants and a local-server context setting that silently truncated long inputs; correcting both avoided distorted comparisons.

Rough edges

The prototype still misinterprets some exception clauses and misses parts of multi-span evidence. Stored results lack authentication and dependencies have known vulnerabilities, barriers to handling confidential NDAs.

Future path

Next, I would test exception reasoning on an independently labeled set, measure retrieval coverage separately from citation completeness, and evaluate longer expert-labeled contracts. Authentication, access control, further adversarial testing and monitoring are prerequisites for deployment.

8Conclusion

The rule-based non-AI baseline established that semantic AI adds substantial value to this task: RAG gained +16.6 risk-sensitive-recall points over Rule. The proposal's separate ≥5-point RAG-over-FULL target was not met; the measured gain was +1.2 points. Against FULL, the stronger quality reference, RAG lowered inference cost while sacrificing some evidence-grounded correctness, and the tested agent and routing policies did not earn their complexity. NDATrace is therefore a bounded reviewer aid whose remaining question is whether people can verify its findings more efficiently without losing accuracy.
