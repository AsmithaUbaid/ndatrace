NDATrace: Does Additional AI Complexity Earn Its Place?

A business case for bounded, evidence-grounded NDA review assistance, tested through controlled engineering experiments.

Ubaidulla Asmitha · Group B

1Business problem and contribution

Enterprise legal-operations analysts such as Tina must check every vendor NDA against a fixed checklist of confidentiality requirements before counsel approves it, a workload that industry survey evidence suggests is substantial [1]. Today the work is done by reading and keyword search, which fails where the commercial risk sits: obligations are paraphrased, qualified by exceptions or split across clauses, so conflicting terms reach counsel unflagged (Table 3), and a missed conflict leaves no record of why it was missed. NDATrace changes that hand-off without changing the authority. Every call arrives with quoted, checkable evidence and a recorded approval or override, so errors become visible and auditable, and the reviewer's task shifts from re-reading a document to verifying a specific claim.

The contribution is a measured business case for bounded assistance: which components justified their cost and which did not.

2Solution and design decisions

The design is governed by one principle: semantic interpretation is placed inside a single rented model call, and everything that must be predictable is kept outside it (Figure 1, Table 1). Retrieval, validation, guards, limits and the review record are deterministic, project-owned software, so the model never validates its own claims. Renting the model avoids training and serving investment but introduces provider dependency; owning retrieval bounds context and cost, but makes an omitted decisive clause a system failure rather than a model failure.

3Experimental justification

The architecture was treated as an investment ladder in which each additional rung had to outperform the one below on a predeclared measure before adoption: keyword rules, a reasoning model given gold evidence, the whole document (FULL), bounded retrieval (RAG), then an agent and automatic routing. I tested reasoning before retrieval because optimising retrieval around a weak reasoner would misdirect spending; even with gold evidence, most contradictions were missed by local models (Figure 2). I then froze prompt and retrieval choices before comparing architectures, so each later difference had one explanation (Table 2).

4Measured results and trade-offs

The decisive comparison was run with model, prompt, evaluator and the official test population held constant (Table 3). The pre-registered success criterion, a five-point gain in risk-sensitive recall over the rule-based baseline, was met by a wide margin (+16.6 points): semantic interpretation clearly justifies its cost over keyword matching, above all for the contradictions that carry contractual risk. Two quality measures are reported because they answer different business questions. Accuracy indicates whether the label is right; Joint correctness additionally requires the cited evidence to overlap the annotated clauses, which is what makes an answer defensible to counsel.

Between the two LLM architectures, the trade-off was unexpected. Bounded retrieval was expected to improve evidence quality by removing irrelevant clauses; efficiency was improved instead. RAG matched FULL on label accuracy and consumed far less context, but FULL retained a significant Joint advantage (74.6% versus 72.5%; Table 3, Figure 4), and because output length barely changed, the cost saving was much smaller than the context reduction suggests. I adopted RAG as the runtime for its bounded, predictable cost and kept FULL as the quality reference. The metrics themselves deserve critique. Span overlap cannot judge legal adequacy; partially cited multi-span evidence is scored as a binary pass or fail at a project-chosen threshold; contestable benchmark annotations are treated as truth; and averaged recall can hide the rare, costly contradiction class, so every class is reported separately.

5Rejected complexity and residual failures

Failure diagnostics suggested a narrow opening for an agent that could search more of the document. I froze a small, cost-capped design before running it and compared three variants (Figure 5). The selective agent never used its tools; when tool use was forced or encouraged, activity rose but no useful recovery followed, and evidence-grounded quality fell. Added spend bought activity, not value. Automatic routing failed a parallel test (Table 2): semantic errors could not be distinguished by cheap signals, so the strongest policy still sent too many cases to review and missed its error target. I therefore kept review of every case mandatory.

The reason is shown by the residual failures (Figure 6). Most failures were interpretive, occurring with the relevant evidence available; retrieval misses were a minority. Case-level diagnosis reveals the mechanisms. The same documented exception clauses were misread by both architectures (cases 034, 039 and 040), and necessary spans were dropped or left uncited by RAG (cases 007 and 043). In case 038, however, a permissive distractor that misled FULL was excluded by RAG's narrower context. Further investment should therefore be directed at exception reasoning rather than at more context or more autonomy.

6Business feasibility and risk

Measured inference is the smallest cost component, dwarfed by the reviewer time needed to verify every result whichever architecture is chosen (Table 4, Figure 7). Once failures are assumed to trigger extra investigation, RAG's cheaper inference is outweighed by FULL's better evidence, so the ranking depends on unmeasured labour assumptions. Under mandatory review, the demonstrated operating effect is a negligible inference cost added to existing review; any saving depends on verification being faster than reading. I therefore claim no savings or ROI; architecture choice should follow evidence quality, not inference price.

Security evidence supports a governance argument (Table 2). In a paired attack study, Joint correctness fell from 85% on clean inputs to 75% under attack: injected instructions hijacked labels, and in one case the attacker's planted text was returned as the quoted evidence. Source validation passed because the quote genuinely existed in the document: validation proves provenance, not trustworthiness. Remediation closed the unbounded-cost exposure and added a pattern guard that quarantines suspicious inputs, but detection, model resistance and human review remain separate, incomplete safeguards; only review is unconditional, which is why it is mandated.

7Critical evaluation and future direction

Evaluation critique

The evaluation supports comparative engineering and investment decisions, not autonomous legal judgment. ContractNLI agreements are public, short and tied to fixed requirements, so any RAG advantage on long enterprise contracts remains untested. Split discipline was imperfect: the test set had been partially observed by a superseded earlier lineage, so it is described as not tuned against rather than blind. Several failure mechanisms are also compressed into the single Joint score.

Difficulties overcome

Two faults that would have silently distorted the headline comparison were caught before the final test run. The original evidence evaluator demanded exact character matches, so correct quotations differing only in line breaks or spacing were scored as failures; it was hardened without fuzzy matching and aligned with the runtime validator. The local inference server was never sent a context-length setting and silently truncated long documents, which would have made the full-context baseline look far weaker than it was; small calibration runs exposed it. Budget gates and frozen protocols before every paid run kept each experiment answering one question.

Rough edges

The prototype's rough edges are concrete. Exception clauses still defeat the strongest architecture; multi-span evidence is cited incompletely; stored results are served without authentication; and some Python dependencies carry known vulnerabilities.

Future path

Priorities follow the failure evidence: first, an independent exception-and-polarity test set, because interpretation dominates failures; second, separate measurement of retrieval coverage and multi-span citation; third, longer expert-labelled agreements, to test whether RAG's evidence losses are justified at scale; fourth, a timed reviewer study recording verification time, overrides and follow-up before any cost claim. Identity, access control, adaptive-attack testing and monitoring are deployment prerequisites rather than accuracy improvements.

8Conclusion

Additional AI complexity did not reliably justify its cost: a rented semantic model earned its place over keyword rules, while bounded retrieval traded evidence quality for predictability and the agent and automatic routing added cost without value. The open business question is whether verifying NDATrace's quoted evidence is measurably faster than reading the contract.
