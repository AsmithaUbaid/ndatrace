NDATrace: How Far Should an Enterprise Go in Adding AI Complexity to NDA Review?

Ubaidulla Asmitha · MSc Enterprise AI, NTU · 2026-10-01

Every escalation in this project — from a keyword rule, to a full-context LLM, to retrieval-augmented generation, to a tool-using agent — was measured against the one before it and kept only if it earned its cost. This report follows that reasoning chain, not a chronology of experiments.

ABusiness problem and the actual gap

Tina, a legal operations analyst, checks incoming vendor NDAs against her company's standard confidentiality requirements before they go to a lawyer — today, clause by clause, because supporting or conflicting language is often paraphrased or placed far from the requirement it addresses. A keyword search misses paraphrase; a general-purpose LLM given the whole document can reason but gives Tina no way to verify where its answer came from. NDATrace is narrower than either: retrieve the clause, classify the requirement as Entailment, Contradiction, or NotMentioned, and show Tina the exact supporting text so she — not the model — makes the call. It does not approve, reject, or negotiate NDAs, and is not scoped to other contract types.

Vendor survey data (LegalOn Technologies, 2025, n=286 — not independently verified) reports 52% of organizations handle 101–1,000 contracts a year at 2–4 hours each; a 30% reduction for a 500-contract team is roughly 450 staff-hours a year. This motivates the problem; it is not evidence this project measured.

BEvaluation design

I evaluate on ContractNLI (607 NDAs, 17 fixed requirements, 2,091 held-out TEST cases: 968 Entailment / 220 Contradiction / 903 NotMentioned), a public benchmark with gold labels and gold evidence spans held out until scoring. Classification accuracy alone is insufficient for an "evidence-grounded" claim: a system can state the right label while citing the wrong clause, or no clause at all. I therefore score Joint correctness — the label is correct and the cited evidence matches the gold span — as the metric that actually means evidence-grounded, alongside accuracy, macro-F1, and per-class recall.

Methodological caveat: the TEST split was not tuned against within this project's own development process (E01–E16 stayed within TRAIN/DEV), but a superseded earlier run did score predictions against it before this reconstruction began — disclosed in docs/data_contamination_register.md. I describe TEST as "not tuned against," not "blind."

CModel and architecture selection

Question: is the model or the retrieval the bottleneck?

An oracle test handed GPT-5-mini the gold evidence directly, no retrieval (n=300): 90.6% macro-F1. Evidence: the model reasons well when given the right clause — the ceiling is set by retrieval and prompting, not model capability, which is where I spent the remaining effort.

Question: which prompt and which retrieval configuration?

A prompt comparison found a minimal, direct prompt (P0) beat two more elaborate variants (label definitions; a decision procedure) on Contradiction recall (22.0% vs 6.0% / 2.0%) and macro-F1 — more instruction produced worse classification, the opposite of my hypothesis, so I froze P0. A retrieval sweep compared BM25, dense embeddings, and hybrid RRF fusion: pre-reranking, hybrid was measurably better, but once a cross-encoder reranker is applied all three converge to the same ceiling (Recall@5 ≈ 92%). I froze BM25 + reranker — the simplest option — because the extra complexity bought nothing once reranking was in the pipeline.

EExperiments that rejected additional complexity

Question: does letting the model search further recover its mistakes?

The selective agent (tools: follow a cross-reference, or pull deeper into the ranked candidate pool) was tested on 15 real escalated cases out of 150. It made zero tool calls across every one — the controller concluded immediately every time — for a net Joint-success change of exactly 0.0 points (one recovery cancelled by one unrelated regression) at $0.0218 of real added spend. This does not show agents never help; it shows that for this trigger and tool set, the extra rung bought nothing measurable, so I did not carry it into the runtime.

Question: can the system reliably flag its own uncertain cases?

I tested four automatic routing policies (n=138) against a provisional ≤40%-review / <10%-residual-error target. The most conservative reviewed only 4.3% of cases but caught just 12.5% of failures; the most aggressive caught 82.5% but required reviewing 51.4% of cases. No policy reached both thresholds at once — deterministic runtime signals could not reliably separate confident-and-wrong from confident-and-right. Rather than ship a gate that misses too much or reviews almost everything anyway, every result routes to a human, who sees the evidence and records a decision (Section F).

FBusiness economics and responsible deployment

Actual prototype. Measured inference cost is $0.00168/case for RAG — not the cost of the workflow, since every result still needs human verification and I have no measured figure for review time, so I make no labor-savings claim. The UI instead reports one illustrative scenario ($3.33/case at 5 min/$40hr, labeled "modeled, not realized savings"). Under it, all-in cost is $0.848/case for FULL vs $0.920/case for RAG — FULL's higher Joint success outweighs RAG's lower inference cost once review cost is priced in, on this dataset, under this assumption. Hypothetical automation — skipping review on high-confidence cases — is not deployed or claimed viable: Section E found no routing policy safe enough to justify it.

Security and human authority. An OWASP-LLM-Top-10 assessment (E21) found prompt injection and unbounded consumption failing; targeted remediation moved unbounded consumption to PASS (length caps, budget ceiling, rate limiting) and prompt injection to PARTIAL (a deterministic guard). A 20-pair robustness study (E16) found clean-vs-attack Joint success drops from 85.0% to 75.0%, and 4 of 11 injection-pattern pairs (36%) still succeeded — injection detection is partial, not solved. There is no authentication; the UI is scoped to public/synthetic NDAs and says so. I implemented a reviewer-decision API this cycle (POST /review/{id}/items/{id}/decision) so Approve, Override, and Reject are persisted against the item they apply to — closing the gap between the standing human-review promise and what the backend previously recorded.

GConclusion

Four things are demonstrated, not assumed: a keyword baseline is insufficient (59.0% accuracy, 50.1% Joint); the model, not retrieval, sets the ceiling (90.6% oracle macro-F1); RAG trades a small, real Joint-quality loss for bounded cost and context; and neither the tested agent nor automatic routing earned its added cost. What remains uncertain: whether RAG's scalability advantage holds on real 50–100 page contracts, whether a shorter evidence list actually reduces Tina's review time, and whether injection detection generalizes past the tested patterns. This is an academic prototype, not a production-ready system — no authentication, no distributed rate limiting, disclosed residual security and generalization risk. Enterprise deployment would need real review-time measurement, longer-document validation, and stronger injection defenses before the cost claims above could be more than scenarios.
