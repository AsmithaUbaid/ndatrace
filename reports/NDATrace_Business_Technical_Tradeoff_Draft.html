Executive finding

FULL-context GPT-5-mini is the strongest measured quality reference on the official ContractNLI TEST set. RAG is retained as the prototype runtime because it reduces input tokens by 50.4%, constrains the evidence surface and offers a more plausible path to repeated review of longer contracts. That selection deliberately accepts a statistically significant 2.16-point disadvantage in Joint correctness. Every output therefore remains subject to human verification; neither realized labour savings nor safe autonomous review has been established.

Architectural decisionClause-aware, no-overlap chunks → BM25 top-20 → cross-encoder top-5 → GPT-5-mini → deterministic parsing, source and security checks → human reviewer. No agent, confidence router or silent FULL fallback is in the final runtime.

1Business problem and success criteria

NDATrace addresses a narrow decision-support problem: helping a legal-operations reviewer decide whether an NDA entails, contradicts or omits each of 17 standard requirements, while returning the clause that supports the conclusion.

The intended user still owns the legal decision. Today, that reviewer searches the agreement, reconciles exceptions and definitions, and records a defensible conclusion. A vendor survey of 286 legal professionals reports that many organisations process 101–1,000 contracts annually and commonly spend two to four hours per review [2]. This motivates the workflow but does not prove NDATrace savings because the study's public methodology is limited and the project measured neither end-to-end review time nor user productivity.

Success was defined at three levels: quality through accuracy, Macro-F1 and Joint label-plus-evidence correctness; risk sensitivity through Contradiction and NotMentioned recall; and operability through latency, tokens, cost, parse validity and source-valid evidence. The original target was at least +5 percentage points in mean Contradiction/NotMentioned recall over FULL. RAG achieved 70.25% versus FULL's 69.07%, only +1.19 points; the target was missed. The official TEST split was frozen for final comparison, but it was not perfectly blind because earlier project work had exposed DEV/TEST data.

3Experimental evidence

The evaluation used ContractNLI: 607 NDAs paired with 17 hypotheses and labelled Entailment, Contradiction or NotMentioned, with evidence spans for the first two classes [1]. The frozen evaluator counted a case as Joint-correct only when the label matched and cited spans overlapped at least 50% of the gold span set; NotMentioned required empty evidence. The decisive architecture test asked whether retrieval could preserve quality while reducing context. FULL and RAG used the same official TEST population (n=2,091), model and frozen prompt; only context construction differed. Figure 2 makes the small label-quality differences visible without truncating the zero baseline; Table 3 adds operational metrics and statistical tests. The result overturns any simple “RAG is better” narrative: its label accuracy is comparable and its risk-sensitive recall is slightly higher, but FULL cites the gold evidence more reliably. Negative experiments narrowed the design. The selective agent's V2 tool-use rate reached 20%, yet no invocation was useful and one was harmful; Joint correctness fell from 75.3% to 68.7% on n=150. Confidence routing also failed: on fresh DEV n=138, the only policy approaching the residual-error target reviewed 51.4% of cases, exceeding the ≤40% workload constraint. These results support omission, not further orchestration.

4Business and technical trade-offs

FULL is preferable when benchmark quality dominates. RAG is retained because it makes context per question bounded, makes retrieved clauses inspectable and reuses a document index across 17 requirements. Those properties are strategically useful for longer or repeatedly queried contracts, but ContractNLI TEST documents (maximum 7,861 tokens) do not prove performance on 50–100-page enterprise agreements. Nor did the experiment show material latency improvement: both paths averaged about seven seconds. Figure 3 therefore plots only measured inference economics and does not imply a throughput benefit.

The original fallback model favours FULL because its higher Joint rate avoids more modeled reviews. However, Joint is an offline correctness metric—not a validated abstention signal—and the prototype requires verification of every result. Labour assumptions therefore dominate while measured inference savings remain small. Build-versus-buy is layered: build the legal workflow, retrieval, evaluation and guardrails that encode project-specific risk; rent hosted reasoning while it materially outperforms local models. This accepts provider dependency, privacy review and variable pricing in exchange for a measured reasoning advantage.

5Responsible AI

The central safeguard is role clarity: NDATrace supplies a classification and traceable quotation, while a human verifies the source, interprets the contract and makes the decision. Runtime controls validate structure and quote provenance, flag instruction-like text, and enforce rate, concurrency and spend ceilings. They do not establish semantic correctness. Figure 4 explains why: most residual errors persist even when retrieval has found the evidence.

The OWASP audit initially recorded three PASS, five PARTIAL and two FAIL categories. Targeted remediation made resource controls pass 17/17 project checks, but injection defence remains PARTIAL; outstanding dependency findings and missing authentication preclude production deployment. Responsible use therefore means supervised evaluation in a controlled environment, not merely placing a “human-in-the-loop” label on the interface.

6Final architectural decision

NDATrace retains the simpler RAG reviewer-assistance architecture and rejects the tested agent and router. The decision is not that RAG is more accurate: FULL's 74.61% Joint correctness is significantly better than RAG's 72.45%. Rather, RAG provides bounded, inspectable context and a 50.4% input-token reduction at a modest quality cost, while the agent added latency, spend and regressions without useful tool actions. Keeping the runtime legible supports debugging and human challenge.

The evidence supports three claims: hosted GPT-5-mini materially outperformed tested local models; FULL is the ContractNLI quality reference; and RAG reduces measured inference tokens and cost. It does not yet support autonomous decisions, realized ROI, reduced reviewer time, production security, superiority on long contracts, or auditable reviewer-decision capture. The next defensible gate is an authenticated pilot with decision logging, representative long agreements, blinded legal review, and measurement of time, disagreement and harm—not another layer of AI orchestration.
