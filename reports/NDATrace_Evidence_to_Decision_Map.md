# Evidence-to-decision map

| Decision stage | Alternative tested | Experiment / population | Recorded result | Reasoning and trade-off | Residual uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Establish a non-AI floor | Majority; keyword Rule | E04B and final TEST n=2,091 | Majority 46.3% accuracy and 0 Joint; Rule 59.0% accuracy, 50.1% Joint, 16.8% contradiction recall | Rules are cheap and deterministic but cannot cover semantic paraphrase and contradiction. Retain only as comparator. | Rule compute is unpriced; other rule systems were not exhaustively optimised. |
| 2. Select a reasoning model | Four models with gold evidence | E01, balanced TRAIN n=300 | GPT-5-mini macro-F1 0.906 and contradiction recall 82%; Qwen 0.638 and 30% | Test reasoning before retrieval so retrieval is not blamed for model errors. Rent GPT-5-mini; keep deterministic controls outside it. | Oracle sample is balanced TRAIN and not a final product estimate. |
| 3. Freeze prompting | Minimal, definitions and procedural prompts; later GPT controls | E03 and E12B/C, separate TRAIN samples | Minimal Qwen prompt led macro-F1 0.507 and contradiction recall 22%; added structure worsened C→NM behaviour; later GPT variants did not justify a change | More instruction can bias the decision boundary. Freeze P0 for controlled downstream comparisons. | Prompt results are model- and sample-specific. |
| 4. Design retrieval | Clause/sentence/fixed chunks; K; BM25/dense/hybrid; reranker | E06, evidence-bearing TRAIN n=4,371 | Reranking reached Recall@5 92.2%; BM25+rerank and dense+rerank differed on 1/4,371; fixed-512 recall was saturated by near-whole-document chunks | Choose clause-256, BM25 top-20, cross-encoder rerank, top-5. Simpler first-stage retrieval ties dense after reranking and avoids a vector index. | Retrieval metrics do not guarantee correct interpretation or citation. |
| 5. Compare architecture rungs | Rule, FULL, RAG | E20 and final artifacts, official TEST n=2,091 | FULL Joint 74.6% vs RAG 72.5%, p=0.0047; RAG input −50.4% and API cost −16.8%; original +5-point RAG-vs-FULL recall target achieved only +1.2 | FULL is the measured quality reference. Retain RAG as a bounded-context runtime trade-off, not as the quality winner. | ContractNLI documents are much shorter than target enterprise contracts. |
| 6. Test wider and dynamic context | Top-11; selective agent; forced full-agent; investigation prompt | E12A and E11, matched TRAIN n=150 | Top-11 +4 Joint points, p=0.263, input +64%. Agent tool exposure rose to 30/150, but useful recovery stayed 0 and V2 Joint fell 75.3%→68.7% | Reject permanent expansion and tested agentic complexity. Behavioural activation alone is not benefit. | Small TRAIN studies; different tools and purpose-built long-document tasks remain open. |
| 7. Decide review authority and safeguards | R1-R3 automatic routing; injection/resource controls | E15 DEV n=138; E16/E21/E22 security sets | R3 reviewed 51.4% and left 10.4% residual error. Four of 11 injection patterns succeeded before targeted controls; simple detector coverage remains incomplete | Make human verification unconditional; retain structural flags, source checks, resource limits and quarantine signals without calling them semantic confidence. | Reviewer behaviour, access governance and adaptive attacks are not fully validated. |
| 8. Frame business value | Inference-only cost; selective-fallback cost; mandatory-verification sensitivity | E18/E20 measured inputs plus declared scenarios | FULL $0.00202 vs RAG $0.00168 per case; output ~727 vs 701 tokens; human-time assumptions dominate and can reverse the ranking | Report measured spend separately from labour hypotheses. Require a timed reviewer crossover before any ROI claim. | Review time, overrides, rework and fixed operating costs are unmeasured. |

This chain explains why the shipped prototype can use RAG while the report still identifies FULL as the strongest measured configuration: product boundedness is an engineering trade-off, not evidence that RAG won the benchmark.
